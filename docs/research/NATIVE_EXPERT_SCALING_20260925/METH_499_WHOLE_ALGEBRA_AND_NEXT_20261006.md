For a new row z=h T^-T and current row-space projector P_e:

    a = z(I-P_e)
    innovation = ||a||_2^2.

algebraic rank, numerical stability, encodability and predictive quality
must therefore be distinguished. The formula is a real linear derivation,
not permission to silently invert/cut off a poorly conditioned matrix.

## 5. Routing is another algebraic problem

For a Cartesian bank with logits z_ij=a_i+b_j:

    argmax_ij z_ij = (argmax_i a_i, argmax_j b_j)
    Z = (sum_i exp(a_i))*(sum_j exp(b_j))
    p_ij = softmax(a)_i * softmax(b)_j.

This gives exact cheap winner AND mass for this logit class. Current LUT/C
checks establish its arithmetic, not that a pretrained arbitrary router
belongs to the class. Dense donor scores contain an interaction residual.
Finite495 optimization still has no near-optimum certificate; class closure
has not been proved.

For ideal logits with uniform score error<=epsilon after a common shift:

    |log(pHat_e/p_e)| <= 2*epsilon.

A winner margin>2*epsilon is sufficient for unchanged winner. These are useful
sufficient conditions, not measured errors for499. Near ties may defeat winner
fidelity even when many functions are good; omitted mass can defeat p even when
the winner is correct. Large n adds choices AND a partition-function problem.

For preserved old logits plus newly added logits:

    p_new(e)=p_old(e)/(1+Z_added/Z_old).

Storing unweighted F separately permits reuse under a changed normalizer.
Preserving useful normalized behavior and pricing the normalizer remain open.
Winner-only routing cannot silently replace the donor's actual contribution.

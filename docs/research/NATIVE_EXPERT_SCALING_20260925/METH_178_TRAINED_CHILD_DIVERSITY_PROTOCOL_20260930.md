# METH-178: trained child-B diversity diagnostic

**Question and evidence.** METH-175 confirms 3,840 matched updates, broad routed support and distinct BF16 rows. METH-176's fresh point estimate favors E12,800, but its source bootstrap fails. Distinct rows alone do not show that the nine content children within a parent learned materially different functions. This postfailure diagnostic reads only the frozen METH-175 candidate/control banks and training route counts; it does not read METH-176 sources or scores.

**Measurement.** SHA-bind both METH-175 result JSONs and BF16 bank files. Stream each of 24 layers in bounded parent chunks, reinterpret BF16 values exactly, and for every parent compute the mean of its nine content B rows. Partition the candidate-minus-control squared weight difference into (a) content-parent mean shift and (b) within-parent child variance, checking the sum-of-squares identity numerically. Report RMS for each part, their fraction of total squared difference, and the same squared differences weighted by the frozen METH-175 content route counts. Record structural-slot difference separately. Verify all 1,280×9 content rows per layer and the total counts, without loading a full 4.404 GB decoded bank into RAM.

**Decision use.** If within-parent variance is negligible relative to mean shift, many changed BF16 rows represent effectively shared capacity and a new expert-learning design needs to create child-specific signal. If it is material, the next question is whether routing to those differences improves actual logits/loss on already consumed diagnostic inputs; weight variance alone cannot prove useful experts. This diagnostic cannot be used to tune on the reserved METH-176 cohort or to promote donor-relative quality. Stop after 15 minutes, above 8 GiB process RSS or on any hash/shape/count mismatch. No GPU, T4 or new model training.

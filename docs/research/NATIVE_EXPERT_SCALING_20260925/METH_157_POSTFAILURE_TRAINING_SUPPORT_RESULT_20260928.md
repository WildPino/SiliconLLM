# METH-157: sparse training support per content specialist

**Finding: most content specialists receive few training selections in the 256-update experiment.** This is a postfailure diagnostic on METH-155's changing-weight route histograms; it does not revisit METH-156 text or rerun a model. The [checker](../../../benchmarks/native_expert_scaling/meth157_training_support_diagnostic.py) binds the METH-155 training and METH-156 quality results by SHA-256. Its [result](meth157_training_support_result.json) is SHA-256 `247fec7974d96105b3ff436b0edc4b4e3d5903f773d31c0c6e5e1a2a62137a29`.

Each layer had 279,912 content selections across 11,520 possible content rows. Of rows selected at least once, the median was only **7–13 selections per row** across layers; the 90th percentile was 57–85. Across all possible content rows, **8,811–9,750 of 11,520 (76.5–84.6%)** had fewer than 32 selections, including 538–2,847 never selected. The fixed structural path's 66,516 selections/layer are separate. This distribution coexists with the passing coverage gate of at least 8,673 content IDs/layer: touching a row and giving it enough varied training signal are different conditions.

The observation makes per-specialist support a plausible limiter of BPB improvement, especially for a 10× expansion trained on the same 256 updates as E1280. It is **not** causal proof: each selected token supplies a dense gradient over the B row, the donor factorization may already be near an optimum, and route quality or objective choice may matter. The shared base preserved control-scale common learning in METH-155, yet METH-156 still failed pooled BPB. Before another large bank run, a new protocol should increase *independent* training contexts per specialist and compare against a control trained for the same added budget, with a new source/fragment-disjoint external set. Repeating the 256 chat continuations would increase counts without equivalent new information. METH-153 and METH-156 are consumed.

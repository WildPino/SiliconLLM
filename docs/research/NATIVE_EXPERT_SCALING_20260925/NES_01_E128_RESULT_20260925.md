# NES-01: trained E32 → E128 native expert-count result

**Verdict: quality BPB, routing and pilot CPU latency pass; greedy-generation
gate fails.** Under the frozen [equal-token protocol](E128_EQUAL_TOKEN_PROTOCOL_20260925.md),
E128 cannot be promoted as useful extra capacity. This is one seed at the
historical L6/TinyStories scale, not pretrained transfer or a 10B/100B result.
The unexpected generative failure stops automatic escalation to E256.

Validation routing at E128 has 36–40% of experts touched over eight positions,
56–63% over 16 and 76–85% over 32, near the independent-selection expectation
for top-8 of 128. E32 touches 84–89%, 95–99%, and 99–100% respectively.
E128 normalized router entropy is 0.904–0.934 versus E32 0.724–0.779; its
top-8/9 probability gap is 0.00075–0.00097 versus E32 0.00270–0.00299.
These are descriptive statistics, not proof of what caused the generation
loops.

During the **free-generated** continuations, neither arm has a dead expert
in the pooled 2,048 positions/layer. E128 max/mean route load is 1.65–2.40
and adjacent selected-overlap 0.051–0.074 (iid reference 8/128=0.0625);
E32 max/mean 1.18–1.48 and overlap 0.240–0.282 (iid 8/32=0.25).
Thus the looping samples do not establish an aggregate routing collapse.
The [free-generation route ledger](nes01_free_generation_routes_20260925.json)
is descriptive; looping can itself change the inputs and routing.

## C artifact and cost detail

Export command:

```powershell
& .\.venv\Scripts\python.exe benchmarks\phase60\e4_export.py --ckpt results\native_expert_scaling\nes01_e128_final.pt --out results\native_expert_scaling\nes01_e128_e4.bin
```

The E128 E4 binary is 394,221,632 B, SHA-256
`259e1aa73593e8fa8f72a997aaa63d30961342676fa0be587a22d920c804d2cf`.
It contains both fp32 reference and packed ternary expert weights for the
parity gate. The C engine is compiled from this branch with clang 21.1.8,
`-O3 -mavx2 -mfma -march=znver2 -fopenmp -DE=32` or `-DE=128` and `-lm`.
The same Ryzen 5 3600X, six threads, byte code, LUT MLP, fast SSM exponential,
3,000 validation input tokens and four invocations per arm were used; the
first invocation was excluded as warmup. The [timing ledger](nes01_cpu_timing_20260925.json)
keeps all raw repeats and the SHA-256 of the local logs. No training or other
CPU benchmark ran concurrently. Desktop noise is visible in one slower
repeat for each arm; the medians are the frozen comparison.

The C compiler's counted ternary expert-code footprint grows from **9 MiB**
at E32 to **36 MiB** at E128. Selected code bytes/token remain
`footprint/E × top-8 = 2,359,296 B` in both arms, before scales, router,
cache-line overfetch or repeated access. Measured median router+selection time
grows **14.4 → 52.1 µs/token**, while the selected-expert LUT path grows
**456.4 → 588.0 µs/token**. Fixed top-k therefore does not make the router
constant-cost, and it did not keep the selected-expert path constant on this
machine. Cache/DRAM attribution is unmeasured; the larger pool and timing
shift alone cannot prove which memory level supplied the bytes. The timed
engine feeds validation IDs into a stateful batch-1 token path; its rate is
not accepted autoregressive generation or 10B-scale throughput.

## Reproduction, samples and decision

`e4_reference.py` now accepts `--ckpt` and `--out-dir`; it rebuilt the E128
PyTorch reference at the correct E. The C `--nes01-greedy-out` mode generates
the 16 predeclared prefixes with either `--mlp fp32 --exp exact` or
`--mlp lut --exp fast`. `--nes01-route-out` optionally records selected IDs
per generated token/layer. The [scorer](../../../benchmarks/native_expert_scaling/score_nes01_greedy.py)
keeps every decoded continuation and token ID: [fp32 samples](nes01_greedy_fp32_samples_20260925.json)
and [LUT samples](nes01_greedy_lut_samples_20260925.json). It counts
overlapping **token** 8-grams within the 128-token continuation, not byte
8-grams or repetitions in the prefix. The
[route scorer](../../../benchmarks/native_expert_scaling/score_nes01_routes.py)
checks the route-file shape, expert IDs and per-token uniqueness.

**Decision:** E128 has a real single-seed BPB gain and a measured pilot C
latency within 1.25×, but the predeclared generation criterion fails in both
fp32 and LUT. Do not infer that E128 scaling preserves useful quality or
escalate automatically to E256. The next quality experiment must change a
specified training/routing variable or per-expert exposure, include the same
free-generation check and measure its additional cost. Independently, probe
CPU router/LUT cost at a **10× larger E** as a throughput-only stress test;
synthetic/duplicated weights there cannot establish quality or pretrained
capacity. Pretrained-to-native transfer and ≥50 tok/s on a quality-valid
10B/100B artifact remain open.

# C50-T2 design draft: auxiliary experts attached to T0, off where T0 is sure

25 October 2026, after addendum 2's PASS ([result](C50_T1_PART2B_FRESH_RESULT_20261025.md)). **A design draft, not a protocol: nothing here is run or chosen.** It is step 2 of decision C (§12, 24
October), "capacity only on the residual the merged LoRA cannot learn, with new experts that act where the original
ones are uncertain, and T0 intact elsewhere". It returns to Phase 1's thesis (ROADMAP §0, §5): stored capacity grows
while the bytes read per token stay at v0, apart from a priced overhead.

**Below, T0 names the base the branch attaches to.** After step 1 that base is v0, T0 with A1 merged: the same
architecture, the same bytes per token. Where a number is T0's own (the anchor rule's 29.4%, the 867 MB per token),
it is marked as such.

## The common shape of every option

At each attached layer ℓ, with h the hidden state entering the MoE block:

    y = MoE_T0(h) + g(h) · Σ_k w_k E_aux,k(h)

- **T0's MoE and router are unchanged:** the same top-8 and the same weights. Nothing competes for T0's slots.
- **The gate g(h) ≥ 0 is exactly 0 by default,** for example relu(u·h + b) with b < 0 at the start.
  - **At step 0 the model is T0 bit for bit.** This is the planted control, as K-none was in part 2b.
  - **Where g = 0, the auxiliary expert is not read.** So the bytes per token grow only on the tokens and layers
    where the gate fires.
- **Training:**
  - the same 33-bucket KL to F0 as S4, plus a cost on how often the gate fires;
  - optionally, a gate target of 0 at the positions where T0 is sure: part 2b's anchor rule, p_T0(y) ≥ 0.5 and
    p_T0(y) ≥ p_F0(y), which held 29.4% of the positions.
- **Gates for the arm:**
  - R on S4's documents, beyond v0's 0.671;
  - the fact panels, not worse than v0, on fresh plain draws, with the donor-gap items reported apart;
  - the controls: remove, shuffle, mask, and a random donor;
  - the engine at the measured fire rate.

## Three ways to fill E_aux

**SOURCE-DERIVED from the configs:**
- **The two models:** T0 is Ling-mini-2.0 (d 2048, 20 layers, 256 experts of 3 × 2048 × 512 = 3.15 M parameters).
  F0 is Ling-flash-2.0 (d 4096, 32 layers, 256 experts of 3 × 4096 × 1024 = 12.58 M).
- **Their tokenizers are the same.**
- **F0's routed experts are about 99.9 B parameters,** roughly 60 GB of its 63 GB Q4_K_M. One F0 expert is about
  7.5 MB at that precision (ESTIMATE from the file size).

| Option | E_aux | Learned | Stored capacity | Training memory | Risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **X: transplant F0's experts** (recommended) | F0's own experts, frozen, behind a trained stitch: h → P_in h (2048 → 4096) → F0's norm, router and top-1 or top-2 → P_out (4096 → 2048) | stitch maps (full: 16.8 M per layer, 319 M for 19 layers; low-rank much less) + gate | up to all of F0's experts: ~60 GB, i.e. ~6× T0's expert bytes. RAM holds ~70 GB with T0, which is tight; a subset of F0's layers, e.g. 16 → ~31 GB | F0's experts must be readable while training: ~1.9 GB per F0 layer at Q4. That is 4-6 layers on T4 × 2 next to T0's 9.9 GB, so an MVE attaches few layers | whether a linear map carries T0's state into F0's input space well enough. Model stitching is reported to work between related networks [L?]; here it is untested |
| N: new experts, trained | full-rank experts of T0's size, from scratch | the experts themselves | bounded by Adam (~16 B per parameter): ~0.3-0.5 B parameters, +2-3% of T0's experts | fits | capacity too small to test the thesis; knowledge only through the KL |
| A′: arm (a), made additive | T0's expert copies + low-rank deltas in the gated branch | deltas + gate | as arm (a) | known (≤ 11.2 GiB per T4) | it is a LoRA on the experts; the gate only fixes the regression |

**Why X:** it is the only option where the knowledge is the 100B's own, carried in its own weights. N and A′ learn
everything through a 33-bucket KL, at a capacity that training memory caps.

## Bytes per token (DESK-MODEL; priced before any run, rule 9)

With top-1 F0 expert per fired (token, layer), on L attached layers and a fire rate p:

    Δbytes per token ≈ p · L · 7.5 MB

| | p = 0.1 | p = 0.25 | p = 0.5 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| L = 16 | +12 MB (+1.4%) | +30 MB (+3.5%) | +60 MB (+6.9%) |
| L = 19 | +14 MB (+1.6%) | +36 MB (+4.1%) | +71 MB (+8.2%) |

These are against T0's 867 MB per token at context 2048. Lower-precision F0 experts would cut them; that is a Phase-2
lever. The fire rate is a measured output of training, not a choice, and it is reported as such.

## The first cell: can T0's state be stitched into F0's? (MVE, local, no training)

Before any T4 time, the cheapest test of option X's risk.
1. **Dump paired hidden states** on about 20,000 positions of S3's validation shards:
   - T0's MoE input at layer ℓ, from the PyTorch forward of S4;
   - F0's MoE input at a matched depth j ≈ ℓ · 31/19, from llama.cpp with an eval callback (a new tool).
2. **Fit P_in by ridge regression** on half of the positions, and test on the other half.
3. **Read the test as F0 reads it:**
   - the cosine between F0's MoE output on the mapped state and on its own state;
   - the overlap of F0's router top-8 between the two.
4. **Planted controls:**
   - F0 → F0 (identity) must give cosine 1;
   - pairs shuffled across positions must give about 0.
5. **Gate:** a threshold fixed in its protocol. If no layer pair stitches above it, option X is closed at linear
   stitching, and the choice falls to N or A′.

This costs one new llama.cpp tool and a few hours of CPU, and it reads no panel.

Every 4th layer of the augmenting model is used | KV arithmetic: anchor 0.0, augmenter 0.7, CALM 84.3. Anchor's own numeric arithmetic 73.7 → 72.0. Code C2T: anchor 30.4, CALM 30.5, anchor fine-tuned 18.9 ("catastrophic forgetting"). | Chen et al. 2025 ([2506.06609](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.06609v3)) | Affine maps between the residual streams of same-family models (Pythia 70m→160m, GPT-2 small→medium, Gemma-2 2b→9b), trained with MSE plus an inversion term; the layer pair chosen by SVCCA | Small model's prefix → map → large model's suffix: next-token CE +27%, +11% and +39% over the large model alone. | PiFi, Kim et al. 2025 ([2506.07424](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.07424v1)) | One frozen Llama-3.1-8B layer plugged into a small model through projections; the small model is fine-tuned on the task | BERT-base average 82.45 → 84.75. | MoE-LPR, Zhou et al. 2025 ([2408.11396](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.11396)) | Original model frozen, new experts added, and a routing-prior loss sending "old" tokens to the original expert | (abstract only, numbers not read) | The design of choice 2 below, "gate off where T0 is sure", has a precedent: a supervised route back to the frozen original |

**What the reading changes in the draft:**
- **Option X is novel in one specific respect: only the donor's experts run, not its trunk.** Nothing read tests that. It is also the part that keeps the speed. The stitching MVE therefore stays first, and it is a real risk, not a formality.
- **Every arm gets a random-donor control**, as in CALM and PiFi: the same stitch and gate with F0's experts replaced by random experts of the same norm.
- **The MVE should report what Chen et al. report**: CE through the stitch, not only the cosine.
